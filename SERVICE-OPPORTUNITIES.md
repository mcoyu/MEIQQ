# MEIQQ service ideas and research context

Director working brief · 19 November 2026

The director's priority is **what the studio can sustainably sell and earn**. Design and print remain relevant, and the partner wants to provide analytics using client business data. The director has confirmed partner analytics experience/tools and access to Chinese-language capability, contractors or supplier relationships. Specific tools, specialties, available hours, supplier categories and commercial terms still need to be attached to a particular offer.

The director wants an open **service ideas launch pad**, not a fixed portfolio to specify before building. Task 1 supplies editable context, blank financial inputs and space to save, compare and research arbitrary ideas. It is implemented in the offline app; release verification is recorded in STUDIO-EXPANSION-PLAN.md. The examples below are hypotheses to change or discard, not evidence of established client demand, available prices or guaranteed earnings. A U.S.-based studio serving local businesses and small brands is one planning context; it is not a market restriction.

## How analytics becomes a sellable service

Start with a buyer decision, such as which products warrant attention, which discount patterns need investigation, or what changed in the sales mix. The deliverable is a short, checked explanation and a bounded next step. A data-readiness result can also be valuable when the available records cannot support the requested conclusion.

An export-based pilot is technically possible without building a new integration platform. Shopify permits report exports that can be opened in spreadsheet tools. That establishes a possible delivery mechanism, not willingness to pay for the service. [Shopify report exports](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/reports-and-analytics/shopify-reports/report-types/custom-reports/export-reports).

Use sales data for supported sales questions. Product-margin work also needs appropriate costs and coverage checks. Shopify's profit reports exclude products without costs recorded at the time of sale, so sales and profit reports can cover different amounts of business. [Shopify profit reports](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/reports-and-analytics/shopify-reports/report-types/default-reports/profit-reports).

A pilot should specify the business/location, reporting period, currency, question, source exports, metric definitions, cleanup limit, review process and exclusions. Separate setup/cleanup from recurring analysis. A monthly offer is appropriate only after both the recurring decision and repeat delivery process are established. Changes following a campaign are observations; attributing those changes to the campaign needs an appropriate evaluation design.

For the initial service, favor aggregate item/sales reports where they answer the question. Agree permitted access and sharing with the client, minimize unnecessary personal information, and scope contractor access to the work. The FTC recommends limiting collection and access and examining service-provider security practices. Cross-border personal or sensitive data work needs its own assessment before delivery. [FTC business information guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/protecting-personal-information-guide-business).

Proposed division of work: the partner leads analytical method and interpretation in their demonstrated area; the director owns commercial scope and client communication; specialists perform bounded production/preparation; a reviewer checks the results. Record each person's actual time, including cleanup, discussion and rework.

## How ideas might reinforce one another

A retailer could purchase a sales diagnostic, choose a merchandising test, commission the relevant product/campaign assets, then review the next period's results. Each stage has its own deliverable and price. Recurring work follows a useful repeated need and a customer agreement, not an assumed automatic upsell.

Chinese manufacturers or exporters needing English-language catalogs, sales presentations or product content are another buyer hypothesis if those buyers are accessible and the team can verify specifications and claims. This is a different buyer segment from U.S. local businesses; investigate its sales process separately.

## Goods and China market boundaries

For goods, compare the complete delivered cost and owner effort with the customer price. Product cost, freight, insurance, tariffs/taxes and other fees can all contribute to landed cost; obtain shipment-specific current estimates rather than a blanket China tariff assumption. Also budget sample, inspection, defect/replacement and coordination effort. [U.S. Commerce landed-cost guidance](https://www.trade.gov/determine-total-export-price).

Supplier research/sample coordination and inventory resale are different models. The proposed first test sells a bounded coordination service, with purchasing responsibilities agreed before an order. It does not assume the studio must finance inventory. Alibaba's sourcing guide describes detailed orders, sample requests and inspection services as parts of the sourcing process; platform participation does not replace your own product assessment. [Alibaba sourcing process](https://activity.alibaba.com/page/how_it_wroks.html).

Selling into China is distinct from sourcing from China. U.S. Commerce describes several distribution routes, including distributors, agents and e-commerce, and emphasizes regional/category focus and suitable partners. The recommended discovery offer would assess one such route for a defined buyer; it would not promise broad market access or sales. [China distribution channels](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/china-distribution-and-sales-channels), [China market-entry strategy](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/china-market-entry-strategy).

## What the Task 1 launch pad supports

Implementation status: delivered on 2026-11-20; see STUDIO-EXPANSION-PLAN.md for verification and limits. The **Studio ideas** workspace is separate from simulator runs. It starts with clearly labeled editable example drafts and supports creating a blank idea, duplication and deletion. Idea families and descriptions remain editable; no business category or example must be adopted.

The **Scope**, **Numbers** and **Research** sections capture buyer/problem, scope, deliverables, inputs, roles, handoffs, checks, price/cost/time assumptions, notes, source URLs and a next action. Research is manual: save questions, findings and links as they arise. Notes remain available offline; choosing a source link opens it in a new browser tab. The app does not automatically search, retrieve, summarize or validate sources, and a saved note is not automatically verified evidence. Example source links describe possible workflows and limitations; they do not validate the user's economics or business fit.

All example financial and time fields are blank. The director supplies estimates in USD, including paid-role hours/rates, other direct spending, owner time and overhead, and can duplicate an idea to compare alternative assumptions. Unknown values stay unknown. Numeric fields represent single estimates; research notes can record uncertainty and the evidence still needed. They are not statistical confidence ranges or market forecasts.

The comparison distinguishes **First engagement** from **Later engagement**, using the same proposed price for both. Later engagement is one delivery with paid-role costs, other direct costs, owner-time allowance and allocated overhead. First engagement adds preparation once: paid-role setup effort, other setup costs and owner setup time. Setup is not amortized across assumed sales; a separate setup charge is not modeled in this release. Different initial or repeat pricing can be explored as separate idea drafts.

Amount remaining after direct spending is distinct from the result after valuing owner time and allocating overhead. **Available per owner hour** is the amount after direct costs and overhead divided by the relevant owner hours, before deducting the owner-time target. It is unavailable when those hours are zero or unknown. Owner-time and overhead allowances are planning deductions, not recorded payments. Count a person's work once rather than duplicating it in a paid role and owner-time allowance. The displayed results exclude taxes and unlisted costs. The interface does not project sales volume or convert a recurring label into contracted revenue. No composite opportunity score or automatically recommended price is produced.

**Studio backup** exports ideas and research in its own JSON format with an 8 MiB limit. **Import ideas** adds validated Studio ideas as new copies without removing the current ideas. Simulator backups remain separate. Example origins and current revision counters are retained in saved ideas; this is not a full audit history or a structured source ledger for every amount. The launch pad does not yet record client agreements, actual jobs, actual payments or cash timing, and descriptive handoffs are not executable simulation workflows.

Task 2 remains proposed: it will test supported service/capacity/cost assumptions using the existing simulator. New analytics roles, inventory behavior or custom handoffs must be marked unsupported or explicitly approximated until the engine models them. The launch pad can describe an analytics idea while the eventual client analysis happens in a spreadsheet or BI tool. Raw client datasets and a general analytics platform remain separate scope.

## Research an idea when useful

1. Start from a blank idea or edit any example. Write the possible buyer, problem and result; leave unresolved numbers blank.
2. Save the next question, relevant notes and source links. When buyer conversations occur, record actual responses rather than assumed conversion.
3. When a delivery exercise is useful, use synthetic or appropriately authorized material and measure specialist work, director coordination, review and rework.
4. Add labor/supplier estimates for the scope being considered. For goods, compare equivalent specifications and delivery responsibilities. Keep a source/reference and its date in the notes.
5. Compare first and later engagement economics, or duplicate the idea to explore another price, scope or delivery method. Decide what to investigate or offer next using both buyer evidence and delivery economics.

This is an optional research path, not a checklist that must be completed to save an idea. The launch pad does not send messages, buy samples, hire contractors, publish offers or share client data.
