# MEIQQ simulator review

November 24, 2026 · Product design, simulation setup and service assumptions · Build `82ace8c`

**Give the owner one short path from “Here’s what I sell” to “What happened this month?”** The restaurant packages are useful examples. The friction comes from opening on somebody else’s service menu, putting two previews above and beside the form, and making the owner work out which numbers describe their business. A few labels also give the wrong impression even when the underlying calculation is sound.

Start with three changes. Give **Set up my business** and **Try the restaurant example** clear entry points. Show which offers the selected customers will actually request. Carry one set of definitions for cash, operating result and owner time from setup through results. Keep the day-by-day engine, project inspector and detailed assumptions available throughout.

## What the starter actually simulates

The new workspace combines a restaurant menu with a regional customer mix of 45% food, 30% retail and 25% local services. The engine chooses a customer group first, then distributes that group's inquiries among eligible services. Every non-food inquiry has just one eligible service. [Workspace initialization](app.js#L10) · [Starter bundle](service-catalog.js#L73) · [Customer mix](presets.js#L10) · [Allocation](engine.js#L171)

| Offer | Entered weight | Eligible customers | Expected share of all new inquiries |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Menu refresh | 40% | Food | 18% |
| 100 table tents | 30% | Food | 13.5% |
| Monthly promotion kit | 30% | All three groups | **68.5%** |

For the kit, 55% comes from non-food customers and 13.5% from food customers. The starter thus directs most new demand into the service that can also generate follow-up campaigns. In the shipped month, five of seven inquiries requested the kit and both accepted jobs were kits. **Keep these services in a named example.** In the mix editor, show the effective share alongside each weight and show how many inquiries go unserved. Let a restaurant-only example explicitly choose food customers. Simply lowering the kit’s weight would still send every non-food inquiry to it while it remains their only eligible offer. [Live assignment](engine.js#L363) · [Campaign scheduling](engine.js#L376)

### One month, two different questions

| Measure | Setup estimate if expected new orders are delivered | Shipped replay at day 30, seed 2417 |
|---|---:|---:|
| New inquiries | 5.28 | 7 |
| Accepted new orders | 1.584 | 2, both delivered |
| Revenue | $814.02 potential | $1,250.00 earned |
| Operating result | +$269.96 | +$444.35 |
| Result after valuing owner time | −$661.43 | −$568.92 |
| Closing cash | Outside the estimate | $6,131.85 from $6,000 opening cash |

The studio earned $1,250, collected $937.50 and incurred $805.65 of costs in this replay. That explains how profit could rise by $444.35 while cash rose by $131.85. Valuing 26.665 owner hours at $38/hour takes the result to −$568.92. **This short reconciliation would make a better first results screen than three margin percentages.** The existing accounting bridge and ledger already supply the detail. [Estimate](engine.js#L164) · [Delivery and payment](engine.js#L329) · [Results overview](app.js#L705)

Across 200 further day-30 replays, changing only the seed from 1 through 200, **80 delivered nothing** and the median operating result was **−$180**. This describes variability under the example inputs. It supplies no evidence of local market demand. Introduce the setup estimate with “If all expected new orders are delivered.” Give the run a date, delivery count and cash balance. A multi-seed range can sit in the analyst view after those two displays are clear.

## Make the assumptions credible at the point of use

**Keep the dated supplier comparisons.** Printyco’s catalog lists 100 table tents at $95, while its own product page lists 100 at $80. A card could say “Supplier allowance · $95 per 100 · public listing · stock, finishing and delivery to confirm.” The $395 customer price belongs on its own line as the studio’s example selling price. A dated supplier quote for the chosen specification can replace the allowance. [Printyco catalog](https://printycomi.com/) · [Printyco table-tent prices](https://printycomi.com/table-tents/)

**Drop unsupported precision from city names.** Choosing Detroit sets 26% quote acceptance and 12-day payment terms. Royal Oak sets 33% and five days. The project’s own evidence register identifies these as low-confidence assumptions. Let location describe the market. Put response rate, terms and customer mix under separately editable business conditions. An owner can still test a slow-paying month or a busy season. Label their duration, because the current “season” multiplier lasts the entire selected horizon. Price changes also hold acceptance fixed unless the user changes it. [Market values](presets.js#L9) · [Conditions](presets.js#L33) · [Evidence limitations](presets.js#L81) · [Price experiment](app.js#L365)

| Assumption to replace | Practical evidence | How to display it |
|---|---|---|
| Outreach yield and acceptance | Qualified inquiries per recorded outreach hour, then wins per decided quote. Keep undecided quotes separate. | Show both denominators, sample size and date window. A new workspace can explicitly use the example estimate. |
| Service mix and print preference | Requested service per inquiry, unserved inquiries, and print choice among accepted quotes where both routes were offered | Show expected effective share in Setup and realized counts in Results. Label 70% managed print as an example until measured. |
| Labor, owner time and revisions | Contractor rate or invoice, plus owner and designer hours for the same unit. Record included rounds and extra rounds separately. | Show the rate, effort and scope together. Identify the owner hourly target as a chosen value. Show which normal delays and additional disruptions are enabled. |
| Supplier, payment and repeat timing | Quantity/specification quote, order and receipt dates, contract terms, invoice due and paid dates, and accepted repeat offers | Keep payment terms separate from actual lateness. Keep a campaign opportunity separate from a sale. Surface the latest relevant observation next to the editable value. |

Use simple source labels such as **Example estimate**, **Your estimate**, **Published comparison** and **Your records**. A manual edit can be a guess. A cited printer price supports one batch specification. Avoid asking the user to approve an entire evidence ledger to enter one assumption. The existing catalog, frozen source records and downloadable evidence can serve the deeper review. [Service evidence](SERVICE-EVIDENCE.md) · [Preset evidence](PRESET-EVIDENCE.md)

**Keep the Planner handoff honest.** The shipped Planner plan has two partners at eight hours each, explicitly includes setup effort and generic direct costs, and carries a combined income goal. The simulator starter has one owner at 15 hours and one designer at 24. These are different scenarios. On entry, name which one is open. Preserve the reviewed copy for supported plans and explain unsupported elements before copying. Replacing a partner with a design slot, dropping setup time or implying that the simulator pays the combined income goal would make the simplified interface less credible. [Planner example](planner-studio.js#L8) · [Handoff checks and mapping](planner-bridge.js#L17)

## Delivery order and acceptance

1. **Correct the decisions people can get wrong today.** Ship the effective mix, paired route economics, agreed deliverable, blank service, acquisition-mode transition, capacity warning and mobile financial figures. Check the exact starter values above and verify that an unfinished service survives reopen and has no effect on a run.
2. **Shorten one complete journey.** A first-time owner should be able to enter one actual service or choose an example, explain the source of their demand, run a month, and find why cash differs from profit. At 390 px and with keyboard alone, verify the financial figures, project details and focus return. Check that one mix error produces one actionable message and that confirming **Simulate first month** reaches day 30 or a genuine funding decision.
3. **Make comparisons dependable before using them to judge changes.** Replay matched and mismatched acquisition histories, including capped outreach and day-zero seed edits. Check both the on-screen and exported comparison. Keep immutable agreed terms, cash-shortfall rollback, explicit funding, same-seed replay, checkpoints, backups and undo. Existing workspaces should reopen with their original terms, menus and numbers. [Commitments](engine.js#L137) · [Packaged regression journeys](build.test.cjs#L20)

For a first user session, ask the owner to build one offer, explain where its next inquiry comes from, and account for the gap between cash and operating result after 30 days. Observe where they pause or open the wrong screen. Those observations would test whether the revised layout actually feels lighter.

## Review evidence and limits

Three reviewers examined interface behavior, service economics and simulation setup. We exercised the working repository’s packaged HTML in its isolated UI test host, traced its engine and responsive CSS, inspected saved browser captures, spot-checked supplier publications and ran all **410 automated checks** successfully. The 200-run sample starts each run at day zero with the shipped configuration and changes only its seed.

The in-app browser blocks navigation to local files, so this review uses the existing screenshots for visual evidence. Fresh browser rendering, assistive-technology behavior and observed owner task completion remain unverified. The project contains no calibration set for the studio’s actual demand or productivity. This is an assessment and proposed design. The simulator implementation and saved workspaces received no changes.
